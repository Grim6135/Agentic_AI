# Paris Art & Gastronomy Companion

## Museum Access and Architectural Landmarks
The Louvre Museum spans 782,000 square feet; reserving early morning or Wednesday/Friday evening entry permits viewing the Winged Victory of Samothrace and Venus de Milo without crushing crowds. The Musée d'Orsay, housed in a former Beaux-Arts railway terminal, contains the premier Impressionist collection worldwide (Monet, Degas, Van Gogh). Climbing the Arc de Triomphe at dusk provides an unobstructed panoramic vista down the Champs-Élysées toward the sparkling Eiffel Tower.

## Gastronomy & Bistronomy on a Budget
While Michelin-starred dining is legendary, authentic Paris shines in neighborhood bistros and bouillon halls (such as Bouillon Chartier or Bouillon Pigalle), serving traditional duck confit, boeuf bourguignon, and escargot for $15–$25 per main course. For budget lunches, obtain crusty baguettes, aged Comté cheese, and fresh jambon from local fromageries and boulangeries for a picnic in the Jardin du Luxembourg or Place des Vosges.

## Arrondissements & Lodging Strategies
The Marais (3rd/4th arrondissements) balances historic 17th-century hôtels particuliers with contemporary art galleries and falafel spots. Montmartre (18th arrondissement) offers winding cobblestone village charm and views from Sacré-Cœur, though the stairs require moderate mobility. Saint-Germain-des-Prés (6th arrondissement) embodies literary cafe culture near the Seine.

## Metro, RER, and Urban Transport
The Paris Metro system is one of the densest worldwide; no point in central Paris is more than 500 meters from a metro entrance. The Navigo Easy card allows loading digital carnets of contactless T+ tickets. RER Line B provides direct rail connection between Charles de Gaulle Airport and central Châtelet-Les Halles in 35 minutes.
