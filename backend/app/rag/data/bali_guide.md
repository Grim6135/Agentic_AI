# Bali Island & Cultural Wellness Handbook

## Ubud Arts, Spiritual Sanctuaries, and Terraces
Ubud represents the cultural and artistic heart of Bali. The Tegallalang and Jatiluwih rice terraces showcase the ancient Subak cooperative irrigation system recognized by UNESCO. Sunrise hiking at Mount Batur (1,717m) begins at 03:30 and concludes with breakfast cooked in volcanic steam fissures. Visitors entering temples such as Tirta Empul must wear a traditional sarong and sash provided at the temple gate.

## Coastal Cliffs, Temples, and Marine Expeditions
The southern Bukit Peninsula features dramatic 70-meter limestone cliffs crowned by Pura Luhur Uluwatu. The Kecak fire dance performed at sunset against the Indian Ocean backdrop requires pre-booked admission. For marine life, day boat trips from Sanur to Nusa Penida and Nusa Lembongan offer snorkeling alongside resident manta rays at Manta Point and views of the iconic Kelingking T-Rex cliff formation.

## Stays and Wellness Culture
Accommodations in Bali range from budget eco-homestays amidst Ubud jungle ridges ($30–$60/night) to boutique wellness retreats and five-star cliffside villas in Uluwatu ($200–$500/night). Traditional Balinese massage and herbal boreh treatments utilize ginger, clove, and rice flour scrubs to relieve fatigue after active trekking days.

## Seasons, Transport, and Environmental Etiquette
The dry season from May through September offers optimal sunshine, lower humidity, and calm waters for diving. The wet monsoon season from November to March features daily afternoon downpours. Hiring a reputable licensed private driver or booking ride-hail apps (Grab/Gojek) is generally safer than renting motorbikes for unexperienced drivers navigating narrow rural roads.
